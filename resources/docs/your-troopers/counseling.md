> [!why] The portal walks you through the wiki procedure, sends its PMs from your own forum account, and fills in the regiment's form for you, so the record is complete, the trooper can answer it, and nobody has to look up the next step.

## DCS and LOR

1. A Developmental Counseling Statement (DCS) is non-punitive: it records a talk with the trooper about their conduct or performance and what is expected next.
2. A Letter of Reprimand (LOR) is punitive: a formal written reprimand that stays on the trooper's record and can lead to further action.

## Who can write and see which

1. A DCS is written by the trooper's chain of command, from their Section Leader up, an acting lead included, and by Admins.
2. An LOR needs command from platoon level up: the Platoon Leader and Platoon Sergeant, the company, battalion and regiment command, an acting lead of a platoon or above, and Admins. A portfolio officer writes a DCS on their staff; an LOR on staff comes from regiment command or an Admin.
3. You never write one on yourself, or on someone whose command you share or who commands you: an Assistant Section Leader does not counsel their Section Leader.
4. A draft is seen only by you, the command above you and Admins. The trooper sees nothing until you share it.
5. A staff grant can read LORs, not DCSs, and never a draft. View as shows only what the previewed leader would see, and changes nothing.

## Start a draft

1. Open the trooper's milpac page (see [Find a trooper](/docs/basics/find-a-trooper)) and choose **New DCS or LOR** in the **Counseling** panel.
2. Pick the **Type** and what it is for: **Missed section practice or roll call** or **Other conduct**. It decides which of the regulations' rules the record is weighed on. Only the types you may write are offered.
3. The draft opens as a case file: the steps on the left, the regiment's form (7CAV-R-042 for a DCS, 7CAV-R-043 for an LOR) filled in beside them. On a phone the steps come one at a time, with the form under **Document**.
4. Choose **Save draft** at any time, from any step. **Continue** saves too. The page shows when it was last saved and warns you before you leave with unsaved changes. Pick the draft up later from the trooper's milpac page.
5. Section 1, the rank, name and billet of the trooper and of you as the authority, comes from milpac. It shows the live values while it is a draft and is fixed when you share.

> [!good-to-know] Before you start, the panel says what flags a new record would earn today, so you can see whether the regulations call for a DCS or an LOR.

## Send the meeting invite

1. In **Meeting invite**, give three times you can meet in the next 72 hours, in Zulu, and choose **Send invite**. The portal sends the wiki's "Meeting: …" PM to the trooper from your forum account.
2. The record shows **Invite sent** and the 72-hour meeting window.
3. For an LOR the invite is required. For a DCS you can skip it when you reached the trooper by voice within 24 hours, or for a positive development session.

## Allow the portal to send PMs

1. The first time you send a PM from the portal, it asks you to **Allow PMs from your forum account**. The forum shows its own screen; agree there and you come back to the record.
2. The permission lasts while you are signed in, for about an hour at a time. When it runs out the portal asks again. It is never stored anywhere else.
3. If the forum does not send a PM, the step does not happen and the record stays as it was. The page says why; try again.

## Write it up and share it

1. After the meeting, fill in **What happened** (the evidenced infraction, previous instances and the regulation, order or SOP) and **The meeting**: the **Date of letter** (the day you met), the notes of discussion, your plan of action, and anything for others. Comments are optional.
2. For an LOR, tick at least one recommended consequence in **Consequence**. For a removal, choose the Section Leader or Assistant Section Leader position, and add any special punishments ordered.
3. Tick that the meeting took place.
4. **Before you can share** lists what is still missing. **Share with trooper** stays blocked until it is all there.
5. Choose **Share with trooper**. Your part is locked, Section 1 is fixed, and the trooper gets the wiki's "After-Action: …" PM from your forum account with a link to the record.
6. An LOR is also sent by PM to the S1 1IC, the S1 2IC and the oversight officer of the trooper's battalion, with a link to the letter.

## Download the form

1. Choose **Download PDF** on the record. The portal fills in the regiment's official form from the record, in your browser, and saves it.
2. Every download is recorded in the audit log. Nothing generated is kept on the server.
3. A record filed with a PDF before the portal wrote them, or brought in by the import, downloads that original PDF instead.

## Withdraw a record

1. Until it locks, you, the command above you or an Admin can choose **Withdraw** on a shared record, with a reason. It goes back to a draft and the trooper is told by PM.
2. A withdrawn record drops the trooper's answer, if any. Share it again when it is corrected.
3. A record cannot be withdrawn once the trooper has asked to appeal it.
4. Only a draft can be deleted. A shared record is withdrawn first.

## Deadlines and locks

1. The trooper answers a DCS until it locks, and an LOR within 24 hours of the After-Action PM. Without an answer the record shows **No response** and stands as written.
2. A record locks 72 hours after you share it. An LOR also locks when its appeal is decided, and never while an appeal is waiting (see [Decide an LOR appeal](/docs/your-troopers/counseling-appeals)).
3. Once locked, nobody can withdraw it, Admins included. Only the Regimental Executive Officer can then overturn an LOR.

## How long a DCS is seen

1. For 30 days after it is shared a DCS is **Active**: the trooper and their chain of command see it.
2. From day 31 to day 90 it is **Inactive**: hidden from the trooper and their Section Leader and ASL, seen by Platoon Leader and up and Admins, and it still counts toward the flags.
3. From day 91 it has **Expired**: seen by Admins only, it no longer counts, and it is kept for good.
4. An LOR stays visible to the trooper and everyone who can see them, permanently.

## Read the flags

1. A record that crossed one of the regulations' thresholds shows a warning under it, such as "Second miss within 30 days of the last: an LOR is called for." The flag stays with the record as time passes.
2. For 30 days after an LOR, a notice at the top of the panel says "No favorable action until" a date.
3. A record from before the portal asked what it is for is weighed as other conduct, and says so.
4. Flags are worked out only from the records you can see, so they never give a record away. A draft counts once it is shared.

## How the rules are measured

The portal follows the counseling rules on the 7Cav wiki (7CAV-R-012, 7CAV-R-014 and 7CAV-R-015). Each record is weighed against the records dated before it, never back from today, and "within" includes the last day.

1. Other conduct: a repeat within 90 days of a DCS calls for an LOR, if it was for the same issue. A repeat within 30 days of an LOR is the one repeat LOR, which goes to an Article 15, again if it was for the same issue. The portal cannot tell whether two records were for the same issue, so the flag says "if".
2. Missed section practice or roll call: a second miss within 30 days of the last calls for an LOR; from 31 days, another DCS. A miss within 60 days of an LOR calls for a second LOR. A second LOR within 60 days of the first goes to an Article 15, company first, then battalion.
3. LORs on any matter: a second LOR within 90 days authorizes removal as SL or ASL, and a third within 90 days goes to an Article 15.

> [!good-to-know] A flag is a reminder of what the regulations call for. It does not start any action by itself.

## Records from before

1. Records filed in the portal with a PDF before it wrote them keep that PDF, and count as shared and answered on the day they were filed.
2. Their fields may be empty. Open the record from the trooper's milpac page: you, the command above you or an Admin can fill them in under **Fill in from the PDF**. The PDF stays as it is.
3. Admins bring in older records from Google Drive links or PDFs (see [Import older counseling records](/docs/admin/counseling-import)).

## Overturn an LOR

1. Once an LOR is locked, only the Regimental Executive Officer can overturn it. A DCS cannot be overturned. They choose **Overturn** on the record and give a reason.
2. The LOR stays on file, marked overturned with the date, by whom and why, and its document can still be downloaded.
3. An overturned LOR no longer counts toward any flag, the no favorable action notice or the forecast.
4. The Regimental Executive Officer cannot overturn an LOR on themselves. Only the Regimental Executive Officer, signed in as themselves, can overturn: nobody can do it through act as, Admins included.

## Troopers who have left

1. When a trooper is no longer serving, their counseling records are read-only for everyone, Admins included. Nobody can start, share, withdraw or answer one, and the panel says so. The Regimental Executive Officer can still overturn one of their LORs.
2. Whoever could see the records still sees and downloads them. If the trooper serves again, the records can be worked on again.

## What is recorded

Starting, saving, deleting a draft, the invite, sharing, withdrawing, the trooper's answer, an appeal decision, an overturn and every download are recorded in the audit log, with the reason where one is given.
