> [!why] The counseling panel keeps each record with its signed document and shows where the trooper stands under the 7Cav regulations, so nobody has to work out the next step from memory.

## DCS and LOR

1. A Developmental Counseling Statement (DCS) is non-punitive: it records a talk with the trooper about their conduct or performance and what is expected next.
2. A Letter of Reprimand (LOR) is punitive: a formal written reprimand that stays on the trooper's record and can lead to further action.

## Who can file and see which

1. A DCS is filed by the trooper's chain of command, from their Section Leader up, an acting lead included, and by Admins.
2. An LOR needs command from platoon level up: the Platoon Leader and Platoon Sergeant, the company, battalion and regiment command, an acting lead of a platoon or above, and Admins. A portfolio officer files a DCS on their staff; an LOR on staff comes from regiment command or an Admin.
3. You never file on yourself, or on someone whose command you share or who commands you: an Assistant Section Leader does not counsel their Section Leader.
4. A staff grant can read LORs, not DCSs, and cannot file. View as shows only what the previewed leader would see, and files nothing.
5. A record you may not see is left out of the panel.

## File a record

1. Open the trooper's milpac page (see [Find a trooper](/docs/basics/find-a-trooper)). The **Counseling** panel lists their records, newest first.
2. Choose **File a record**.
3. Pick the **Type**: Developmental Counseling Statement (DCS) or Letter of Reprimand (LOR). Only the types you may file are offered.
4. Set the **Date** the counseling was given. It cannot be in the future.
5. Under **What it is for**, choose **Missed section practice or roll call** or **Other conduct**. It decides which of the regulations' rules the record is weighed on, and it cannot be changed later.
6. Write the **Reason**.
7. Attach the **Signed document**: a PDF of 10 MB or less.
8. Choose **File record**.

> [!good-to-know] Before you file, the form says what flags the new record would earn with the date and purpose you chose. Filing the same record twice within 10 minutes is refused as already filed, so a double click never counts twice.

## Read the flags

1. A record that crossed one of the regulations' thresholds shows a warning under it, such as "Second miss within 30 days of the last: an LOR is called for." The flag stays with the record as time passes.
2. For 30 days after an LOR, a notice at the top of the panel says "No favorable action until" a date.
3. A record filed before the portal asked what it is for is weighed as other conduct, and says so.
4. Flags are worked out only from the records you can see, so they never give a record away.

## How the rules are measured

The portal follows the counseling rules on the 7Cav wiki (7CAV-R-012, 7CAV-R-014 and 7CAV-R-015). Each record is weighed against the records dated before it, never back from today, and "within" includes the last day.

1. Other conduct: a repeat within 90 days of a DCS calls for an LOR, if it was for the same issue. A repeat within 30 days of an LOR is the one repeat LOR, which goes to an Article 15, again if it was for the same issue. The portal cannot tell whether two records were for the same issue, so the flag says "if".
2. Missed section practice or roll call: a second miss within 30 days of the last calls for an LOR; from 31 days, another DCS. A miss within 60 days of an LOR calls for a second LOR. A second LOR within 60 days of the first goes to an Article 15, company first, then battalion.
3. LORs on any matter: a second LOR within 90 days authorizes removal as SL or ASL, and a third within 90 days goes to an Article 15.

> [!good-to-know] A flag is a reminder of what the regulations call for. It does not file anything or start any action by itself.

## Correct or delete a record

1. Choose **Correct** on a record to change its date or reason. Give a **Reason for the correction** and choose **Save correction**. The type and the document stay as filed; to change them, delete the record and file it again.
2. Choose **Delete** to remove a record and its document from the portal. Give a **Reason for deleting** and choose **Delete record**.
3. The leader who filed it, the other leaders of the unit they filed it through, and the command above can correct or delete it, as long as they still counsel the trooper. A record an Admin filed is changed by Admins only.
4. An LOR can be corrected or deleted only in the 72 hours after it is filed, its appeal window: the trooper has 24 hours to say they appeal, and the appeal is done within 72. After that nobody can correct or delete it, Admins included. A DCS can be changed at any time.
5. Filing, correcting, deleting and overturning are all recorded in the audit log, with the reason.

## Overturn an LOR

1. Once an LOR is past its 72 hours, only the Regimental Executive Officer can overturn it. They choose **Overturn** on the record, give a **Reason for overturning** and choose **Overturn record**.
2. The LOR stays on file. It is shown muted with "Overturned on" the date, by whom and why, and its document can still be downloaded.
3. An overturned LOR no longer counts toward any flag, the no favorable action notice or the filing form's warnings.
4. The Regimental Executive Officer cannot overturn an LOR filed on themselves. Only the Regimental Executive Officer, signed in as themselves, can overturn: nobody can do it through act as, Admins included.

## The Google Drive copy

Each record shows where its copy on the 7Cav Google Drive stands:

1. **Drive copy pending**: the copy is queued.
2. **Copied to Drive**: the document is in the 7Cav Google Drive folder.
3. **Drive copy failed**: the copy did not go through. It is tried up to 5 times, with pauses of 1, 5, 15 and 60 minutes. The record and its document are kept in the portal either way.
4. **Portal only**: Google Drive is not set up, so the document is kept in the portal only.

> [!good-to-know] Deleting a record in the portal leaves a copy already on Google Drive where it is.

## Troopers who have left

1. When a trooper is no longer serving, their counseling records are read-only for everyone, Admins included. Nobody can file, correct or delete a record, and the panel says so. The Regimental Executive Officer can still overturn one of their LORs.
2. Whoever could see the records still sees and downloads them. If the trooper serves again, the records can be changed again.
